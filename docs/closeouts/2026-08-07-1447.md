## Decisions Made

Five design questions were put to the user and answered:

1. **Separate tool, not a mode** in the existing `/pleading-paper/` page.
   Distinct search intent ("add line numbers to existing PDF" vs "pleading
   paper generator"), and folding it in would roughly double an already
   796-line file.
2. **Manual alignment with live preview**, not auto-detection. Always works,
   including on scanned/image-only PDFs with no text layer. Auto-suggest from
   text baselines was considered and deferred as a later enhancement.
3. **Scan all pages for gutter collisions, warn, never block.** The
   single-page preview cannot catch a collision on page 17.
4. **Draw numbers + vertical rules, rules toggleable** (default on). Rules are
   the element most likely to strike through text on a document with a 1"
   left margin.
5. **All pages by default, with an optional range field**, reusing
   `parsePageRange` from `page-extractor/index.html:365`.

Two further decisions worth recording because they are non-obvious:

- **Overlay only — deliberately NOT reusing `bates-stamp`'s existing
  `scaleContent`/`translateContent` whitespace-border machinery.** Shrinking
  or shifting page content to open up a left gutter can silently push a
  conforming brief out of compliance with court font-size and margin rules
  (CRC 2.104/2.107 and most federal local rules). A tool that quietly makes a
  filing non-conforming is worse than one that declines the job. The correct
  response to a crowded gutter is to warn, not to mangle.
- **Line spacing must be an explicit input, not derived from margins.** The
  existing blank generator uses `usableHeight / lineCount`, which is correct
  when it owns the page. In overlay mode the spacing must match the *source
  document's* leading (typically 24pt for double-spaced 12pt). Deriving it
  from margins would guarantee misalignment. This is the single easiest way
  to get the tool subtly and invisibly wrong.
